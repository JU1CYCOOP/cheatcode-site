He Weighed 400 Pounds and Wouldn't Give Up Fast Food. So He Learned How to Order It.

Greg Cooper lost 225 pounds by ordering differently at the drive-thru. His new app, CheatCode, does the math for everyone else.

November 16, 2026 — At his heaviest, Greg Cooper weighed 400 pounds. Every diet he tried asked him to stop eating out, and every one of them ended the same way: back in the drive-thru line.

So he stopped trying to quit. Instead, he started reading the nutrition charts restaurants publish and working out what to actually say at the counter. A Subway roast beef with no mayo and every free vegetable on the line. Chicken fingers from Raising Cane's with hot sauce in place of the house sauce. He called these orders cheat codes. Today Cooper weighs 175 pounds and lifts, and he still eats out most days.

This week he released those orders as CheatCode, an iPhone app now on the App Store.

"I never had the willpower to live on chicken and broccoli," Cooper said. "What worked was knowing exactly what to order at the places I was already going. I figured it out one restaurant at a time. Now anyone can do it in ten seconds."

CheatCode asks for a calorie and protein goal, or works one out from a few questions. It then scores every order at the restaurants nearby for how well it fits. There are nearly 500 cheat codes across more than 50 chains, including Chipotle, Chick-fil-A, Starbucks, Taco Bell and Olive Garden. Each one is a real order built from the regular menu, with step-by-step wording to read out at the counter. If someone needs a bigger meal, the app moves them up to a size the restaurant really sells, so a 6-inch becomes a Footlong rather than "one and a half sandwiches."

The numbers come from each restaurant's own published nutrition information, and every order shows where they came from. When a chain doesn't publish something, like a pump of syrup or a splash of milk, CheatCode uses the USDA figure and labels it an estimate.

"Everyone asks if it's AI," Cooper said. "It isn't. If Chipotle says a scoop of chicken is 180 calories, that's the number you see."

CheatCode doesn't ask users to log meals or make an account, and it has no ads. It is available on iPhone for $29.99 a year after a three-day free trial, or $9.99 a month.
