What is CheatCode?

CheatCode is a new iPhone app that tells people exactly what to order at more than 50 restaurant chains to fit their calorie and protein goals.

What problem does it solve?

Restaurants publish their nutrition information, but a nutrition chart doesn't say what to order. CheatCode does, with the exact words to say at the counter.

What does that look like?

Nearly 500 custom orders built from regular menu items, a match percentage on every order, and orders that resize to each person using only portions the restaurant sells.

Why does it matter?

You don't have to stop eating what you love to make progress toward your goals. You just need to know what to order.

When is it available?

CheatCode is available now for iPhone on the App Store.

The drive-thru just got a game plan.

For anyone cutting calories, building muscle or simply trying to eat better, restaurant meals have always meant guessing. CheatCode replaces the guess with an answer.

Set a calorie and protein target once, and the app turns the menus at more than 50 national chains, from Chipotle and Chick-fil-A to Starbucks and Olive Garden, into orders that fit. Every order carries a match percentage showing how closely it lines up with those numbers, and the best matches come first.

It is built for the moment before the meal, not after it. Open it in the parking lot or the line, pick an order, and read it out.

“For a long time, staying in shape meant I just didn't go to fast food places, even when my friends did,” says Greg Cooper, founder of CheatCode. “I built CheatCode so nobody has to make that trade. Whether you're out with a friend or just need something quick, there should be an order that works for you.”

Cheat Codes: Custom Orders Built Around Your Macros

CheatCode's nearly 500 cheat codes are custom orders built from regular menu items: a Subway roast beef with no mayo and every free vegetable on the line, a Chipotle bowl with a second scoop of fajita veggies, chicken fingers with hot sauce in place of the house sauce.

Each one comes with step-by-step instructions, written in the restaurant's own words, so the order is as easy to say as it is to read.

When someone needs more or less food, the order resizes using only portions the restaurant actually sells. A 6-inch becomes a Footlong. An 8-piece becomes a 12.

“A cheat code is just a smarter way to order something you already like,” says Cooper.

Match Percentage: Every Order, Measured Against You

Two people can walk into the same restaurant and need completely different meals. CheatCode scores every order against each user's own calories and protein, and plans for high-protein, low-fat, keto and vegetarian eating change the results.

Alongside the cheat codes, a second list ranks regular menu items that fit as they are, with nothing to change at the counter.

Make It Yours: Every Order, Adjusted in a Tap

Any order can be made lighter or more filling, sized up or down, given a different side or milk, or topped up with more protein for the fewest extra calories, with the numbers updating as it changes.

Orders combine into a plate that shows the total and one set of instructions for everything. Budget-friendly orders that use only free changes, seasonal and trending drink orders, and treats are each clearly marked. Nearby restaurants appear closest first, with estimated drive times.

“Nobody orders exactly what's on the board,” says Cooper. “The app shouldn't make you.”

Restaurant Numbers: Sourced, Not Guessed

Every figure in CheatCode comes from the restaurant's own published nutrition information, and the source is shown on every order.

When a restaurant does not publish a part, such as a pump of syrup or a splash of milk, the app uses U.S. Department of Agriculture data and labels it as an estimate.

“If a restaurant publishes a number, that's the number you see,” says Cooper. “If it doesn't, we say so.”

Together, these pieces change when nutrition decisions happen. Traditional calorie apps ask people to log a meal after it's eaten. CheatCode helps them choose it before they order, at the places they were already going.

CheatCode is available now for iPhone on the App Store, at $29.99 per year after a three-day free trial, or $9.99 per month. It requires no account and shows no ads.

How CheatCode Started

For a long time, getting healthy meant Greg Cooper stayed away from fast food completely.

He was around 220 pounds when he decided to make a change, and the advice was always the same: cut out fast food. So he did. When friends asked if he wanted to grab a bite, he usually said no. When he was busy and the quick option was right there, he drove past it. It worked, but it never felt like something he could keep doing forever.

“I didn't want to be the guy who sits out every time someone says, ‘let's grab food,’” Cooper says. “I just wanted to know what I could actually order.”

So he started digging. He read through restaurants' nutrition guides, figured out which orders fit his calories and protein, and wrote down exactly how to ask for each one. Leave off a sauce here, add a free vegetable there, pick the size that fits. He started calling them cheat codes.

He got down to 175 pounds and has stayed there, without skipping the places everyone else was eating.

That's why he built CheatCode. Whether you're meeting a friend or just need something fast on the way home, there should be something on the menu that works for you, at every chain in the app.
