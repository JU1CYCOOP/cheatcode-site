FOR IMMEDIATE RELEASE

New iPhone App CheatCode Turns Restaurant Menus Into Orders That Fit Your Calories and Protein

Built on each chain's own nutrition data, CheatCode gives diners a ready-made order, sized to their goals, and the exact words to say at the counter.

November 16, 2026 — CheatCode today launched its iPhone app for people who eat out and still want to hit their calorie and protein goals. CheatCode covers more than 50 national restaurant chains, from Chipotle and Chick-fil-A to Starbucks and Olive Garden, and is available now on the App Store.

Eating out is no longer the exception. More than half of U.S. food spending now goes to food away from home, a record 58.9% in 2024 according to the USDA Economic Research Service, and the CDC reports that about one in three U.S. adults eats fast food on any given day. Yet the nutrition information restaurants publish is usually buried in long PDF tables that say nothing about what to actually order.

CheatCode closes that gap. A user enters their calorie and protein targets once, or lets the app work them out from a short setup. From there, every order in the app carries a match percentage for that person, and the best matches come first.

Two ways to order

  • Cheat codes: nearly 500 custom orders built from regular menu items, such as a burrito bowl with a second scoop of free fajita veggies or a sandwich ordered without the mayo. Each one sizes itself to the user's meal, using only sizes the restaurant actually sells: 8 nuggets or 12, a 6-inch or a Footlong.
  • Straight off the menu: regular menu items picked to hit the user's numbers, with nothing to change at the counter.

Numbers from the restaurant, not a guess

Every figure in CheatCode comes from the restaurant's own published nutrition information, and each order shows its source. When a restaurant does not publish a component, such as a pump of syrup or a splash of milk, CheatCode uses the USDA FoodData Central figure and clearly marks it as an estimate.

"Most nutrition apps ask you to log what you already ate. I wanted something that tells you what to order before you get to the counter," said Greg Cooper, founder of CheatCode. "The numbers come straight from the restaurant, and the app gives you the exact words to say, so ordering a meal that fits your goals takes about ten seconds."

Built for the counter and the drive-thru

Users can make any order lighter or tastier, size it up or down, swap a side, change the milk in a coffee, or add protein for the fewest extra calories. Adding orders to a plate shows the total and a step-by-step script for ordering each item. The app also includes build-your-own tools at chains such as Subway, Chipotle, Domino's and Starbucks; the viral and seasonal drink orders circulating on social media, rebuilt with real numbers; and labels for Budget friendly, Lean, High volume, Seasonal and Treat pick orders. Restaurants are listed closest first, and filters cover keto, vegetarian, high-protein and low-fat diets.

Private by design

CheatCode requires no account and shows no ads. The app collects no personal data. Location is optional and used on the device only to sort nearby restaurants.

Availability

CheatCode is available now on the App Store for iPhone at https://apps.apple.com/app/id6832425448. It is offered as a subscription: $29.99 per year with a 3-day free trial for new subscribers, or $9.99 per month. More information is at https://getcheatcode.app.

About CheatCode

CheatCode is an iPhone app that helps people who eat out choose restaurant orders that fit their calorie and protein goals, using each restaurant's own published nutrition data.
